We examine when a LATE estimated in one population predicts the LATE in another. We decompose the prediction error of 2SLS with interacted fixed effects into an in-sample estimand error and two external terms tied to slope error and nonlinear treatment effects. We show that Double/Debiased Machine Learning improves prediction when the nuisance functions are misspecified, but its performance under nonlinear treatment effects depends on the final-stage model.
